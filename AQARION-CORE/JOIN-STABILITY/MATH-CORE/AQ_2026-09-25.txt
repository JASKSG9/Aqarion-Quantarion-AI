1. FINITE JOIN-STABILITY THEOREM
======================================================================

Let X be finite.

Let T : X -> X.

Let E,F be equivalence relations on X.

Assume:

    T(x) E T(y) -> x E y

and:

    T(x) F T(y) -> x F y.

Then:

    T(x) (E join F) T(y) -> x (E join F) y.

Equivalently:

    T^(-1)(E join F) subset E join F.

Proof method:

    E-classes
    F-classes
    incidence graph
    induced permutations
    component preservation.

Evidence status:

    [P]

2. ARBITRARY-SET COUNTEREXAMPLE
======================================================================

Let:

    X = N
    T(n) = n + 1.

Let E have non-singleton class:

    {0,2}

and otherwise singleton classes.

Let F have non-singleton class:

    {0,3}

and otherwise singleton classes.

Then E and F are pullback-stable.

But:

    2 E 0 F 3

so:

    2 (E join F) 3.

Meanwhile:

    1 not (E join F) 2.

Since:

    T(1)=2
    T(2)=3,

we have:

    T(1) (E join F) T(2)

but:

    1 not (E join F) 2.

Therefore:

    T^(-1)(E join F) is not a subset of E join F.

The unrestricted theorem is FALSE.

Evidence status:

    [F]

4. RANGE-GAP LEMMA
======================================================================

Let:

    G = E join F.

If E and F are pullback-stable but G is not, then a G-path witnessing
the failure between image points must contain an internal vertex outside
im(T).

If every G-path between image points can be replaced by a path entirely
inside im(T), the obstruction disappears.
