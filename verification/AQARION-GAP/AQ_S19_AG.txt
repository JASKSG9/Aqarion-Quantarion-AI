*Proof.* There are only five partitions of a 3-set. Direct inspection of the join table shows that every other combination produces either the discrete partition (\(c_S=3\)) or ALL3 (\(c_S=1\)).

**Case 1 — both local types PAIR.**  
Then \(\tau(M)=\mathrm{PAIR}\), so \(d_T(M)=1\) and \(M\) is not SEP-pure. Also \(d_T(P)=d_T(Q)=1\). Hence
\[
\operatorname{diff}=1+1-1-1=0.
\]
The target rule likewise predicts \(0\) (meet is not SEP-pure).

**Case 2 — one side PAIR, one side discrete (SEP).**  
Without loss of generality \(\tau(P)=\mathrm{PAIR}\) and \(\tau(Q)=\mathrm{SEP}\) (discrete). Then \(\tau(M)=\mathrm{SEP}\) (discrete), \(d_T(P)=1\), and
\[
d_T(Q)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if \(Q\) SEP-pure},\\2&\text{if \(Q\) SEP-ext},\end{cases}
\qquad
d_T(M)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if \(M\) SEP-pure},\\2&\text{if \(M\) SEP-ext}.\end{cases}
\]

Subcases:

- \(Q\) SEP-pure.  
  The blocks of \(Q\) on \(S\) are exactly \(\{0\},\{1\},\{2\}\). Meeting with \(P\) therefore yields
  \[
  M_s=P_s\cap\{s\}=\{s\}\qquad(s\in S),
  \]
  so \(M\) is SEP-pure as well. Then
  \[
  \operatorname{diff}=1+0-0-1=0.
  \]
  The target rule also predicts \(0\) because one input is SEP-pure.

- \(Q\) SEP-ext and \(M\) SEP-pure.  
  Then
  \[
  \operatorname{diff}=1+2-0-1=2.
  \]
  Here \(M\) is SEP-pure while neither \(P\) (PAIR) nor \(Q\) (SEP-ext) is SEP-pure, so the target rule predicts \(2\).

- \(Q\) SEP-ext and \(M\) SEP-ext.  
  Then
  \[
  \operatorname{diff}=1+2-2-1=0.
  \]
  The target rule predicts \(0\) because the meet is not SEP-pure.

(Note: if \(Q\) is SEP-pure then \(M\) is forced SEP-pure, so the combination “\(Q\) pure and \(M\) ext” cannot occur.)

**Conclusion for \(c_S=2\).**  
In every admissible local type pair the reduced formula
\[
\operatorname{diff}=d_T(P)+d_T(Q)-d_T(M)-1
\]
evaluates to exactly
\[
\begin{cases}
2 & \text{when \(M\) is SEP-pure and neither \(P\) nor \(Q\) is SEP-pure},\\
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
No appeal to \(n\), to global external structure beyond the already-proved \(d_T\)-table, or to enumeration of partitions of \(X\) is required: the argument uses only the five-element lattice of partitions of \(S\), the order relation \(\tau(P)\vee\tau(Q)\preceq\tau(U)\), and the four-way \(d_T\) values.

Together with the previously closed branches

- \(c_S=1\) (forces \(\tau(U)=\mathrm{ALL3}\), \(d_T(U)=0\), \(c_G=1\)),
- \(c_S=3\) with at least one of \(P,Q\) SEP-pure (forces \(M\) SEP-pure),

this completes the algebraic analysis of the gap on the \(c_S=2\) locus. The sole remaining open branch for a fully inductive all-\(n\) theorem is the subcase \(c_S=3\) in which *both* inputs are SEP-ext; that branch still requires a direct description of \(d_T(U)-c_G\) under external bridging among three locally discrete components.

d_T(U)=2=c_G-1.

### 0. Scope and non-claims

This note formalises one identity that arose in the AQ-S17 / partition-lattice work:

\[
\Delta_d(P,Q)-(c_S-c_G)
=
\begin{cases}
2 & \text{if \(M\) is SEP-pure and neither \(P\) nor \(Q\) is SEP-pure},\\
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

It is **not** a claim about Kaprekar dynamics, residual Gram operators, Smith forms, Lean certificates, or publication readiness. Those remain separate threads.

---

### 1. Fixed data

Let \(n\ge 3\) and \(X=\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}\).  
Fix the distinguished support
\[
S=\{0,1,2\}
\]
and the permutation
\[
T=(0\ 1\ 2)
\]
extended by the identity on \(X\setminus S\). Write \(\mathcal{P}(X)\) for the lattice of set partitions of \(X\) (meet = common refinement via nonempty block intersections; join = transitive closure of the union of the corresponding equivalence relations).

For \(\Pi\in\mathcal{P}(X)\) let \(\tau(\Pi)\) be the restriction of \(\Pi\) to \(S\) (a partition of the three-point set). Define the **orbit join**
\[
J_T(\Pi)=\Pi\vee T(\Pi)\vee T^2(\Pi)
\]
and the **defect**
\[
d_T(\Pi)=\lvert\Pi\rvert-\lvert J_T(\Pi)\rvert.
\]

---

### 2. Local types on \(S\)

A partition of \(S\) falls into one of four classes:

| Class | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| ALL3 | single block \(\{0,1,2\}\) |
| PAIR | exactly one doubleton and one singleton (three labelled possibilities) |
| SEP | three singletons (the discrete partition) |

A global partition \(\Pi\) is called

- **SEP-pure** when \(\tau(\Pi)\) is discrete **and** the three blocks of \(\Pi\) that meet \(S\) are exactly the singletons \(\{0\}\), \(\{1\}\), \(\{2\}\);
- **SEP-ext** when \(\tau(\Pi)\) is discrete **and** at least one of those three blocks properly contains an element of \(X\setminus S\).

(The classes ALL3 and PAIR need no purity distinction for \(d_T\).)

---

### 3. Lemma (four-way evaluation of \(d_T\))

For every \(\Pi\in\mathcal{P}(X)\) and every \(n\ge 3\),

\[
d_T(\Pi)
=
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if \(\tau(\Pi)\) is ALL3},\\
1 & \text{if \(\tau(\Pi)\) is PAIR},\\
0 & \text{if \(\Pi\) is SEP-pure},\\
2 & \text{if \(\Pi\) is SEP-ext}.
\end{cases}
\]

*Proof (by direct orbit computation).*

- **ALL3.** The unique block containing \(S\) is invariant under \(T\). No further merges occur on the orbit, so \(J_T(\Pi)=\Pi\) and \(d_T=0\).
- **PAIR.** Without loss of generality the doubleton is \(\{0,1\}\). The three orbit images produce exactly one additional identification that merges the singleton \(\{2\}\) into the same component; the net block-count drop is \(1\).
- **SEP-pure.** The three singleton blocks are merely permuted by \(T\). Hence \(T(\Pi)\) and \(T^2(\Pi)\) have the same block set as \(\Pi\), so \(J_T(\Pi)=\Pi\) and \(d_T=0\).
- **SEP-ext.** At least one block, say \(B_0=\{0\}\cup E\) with \(E\neq\emptyset\), carries external points. Its images are \(E\cup\{1\}\) and \(E\cup\{2\}\). Shared membership of \(E\) chains the three local blocks into a single block of \(J_T(\Pi)\). The local contribution therefore drops from three blocks to one, a net drop of \(2\). (Attachments on more than one local block produce the same collapse.)

The argument never uses the cardinality of \(X\setminus S\) beyond the existence or non-existence of a nonempty external set \(E\). Thus it holds for every \(n\ge 3\).

---

### 4. Auxiliary lattice facts

**Lemma (meet commutes with restriction).**  
\[
\tau(P\wedge Q)=\tau(P)\wedge\tau(Q).
\]
*Proof.* Blocks of the meet are nonempty intersections of blocks; restricting those intersections to \(S\) yields the same equivalence relation on \(S\) as first restricting and then intersecting.

**Non-identity.** In general
\[
\tau(P\vee Q)\;\ne\;\tau(P)\vee\tau(Q).
\]
External elements may create bridges, so the global join can be strictly coarser on \(S\). The correct relation is only
\[
\tau(P)\vee\tau(Q)\;\preceq\;\tau(P\vee Q).
\]

**Notation.** Write
\[
M=P\wedge Q,\qquad U=P\vee Q,
\]
\[
c_S=\lvert\tau(P)\vee\tau(Q)\rvert,\qquad
c_G=\lvert\tau(U)\rvert.
\]
Always \(c_S\ge c_G\). The gap is
\[
\operatorname{diff}(P,Q)
=
\Delta_d(P,Q)-(c_S-c_G),
\]
where
\[
\Delta_d(P,Q)=d_T(P)+d_T(Q)-d_T(M)-d_T(U).
\]

---

### 5. Main theorem

**Theorem (gap identity).**  
For all partitions \(P,Q\) of \(X\) (\(n\ge 3\)) and the fixed 3-cycle action above,
\[
\operatorname{diff}(P,Q)
=
\begin{cases}
2 & \text{if \(M\) is SEP-pure and neither \(P\) nor \(Q\) is SEP-pure},\\
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

*Proof by exhaustive local-join cases on \(c_S\in\{1,2,3\}\).*

---

#### Case \(c_S=1\)

Then \(\tau(P)\vee\tau(Q)=\mathrm{ALL3}\). The global join cannot separate points already identified, so
\[
\tau(U)=\mathrm{ALL3},\qquad d_T(U)=0,\qquad c_G=1.
\]
Hence \(c_S-c_G=0\) and
\[
\operatorname{diff}=d_T(P)+d_T(Q)-d_T(M).
\]
(The identity now depends only on the three local types of \(P\), \(Q\) and \(M\).)  
Whenever the meet is SEP-pure while both inputs fail to be SEP-pure, the four-way table forces the numerical value \(2\); in every other combination it forces \(0\). (Explicit verification is finite: only the five partitions of a 3-set need be examined.)

---

#### Case \(c_S=2\)

Then \(\tau(P)\vee\tau(Q)\) is a PAIR partition of \(S\). Consequently \(\tau(U)\) is either that same PAIR or ALL3.

**Cancellation lemma.**  
\[
d_T(U)-c_G=-1.
\]
Indeed:

- \(\tau(U)=\mathrm{PAIR}\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(d_T(U)=1\), \(c_G=2\);
- \(\tau(U)=\mathrm{ALL3}\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(d_T(U)=0\), \(c_G=1\).

In both subcases \(d_T(U)=c_G-1\). Substituting \(c_S=2\) yields the reduced formula
\[
\operatorname{diff}=d_T(P)+d_T(Q)-d_T(M)-1.
\]

**Possible local type pairs.**  
Up to labelling, the only pairs of partitions of \(S\) whose join is a fixed PAIR are:

1. both sides equal to that PAIR;
2. one side that PAIR and the other the discrete partition.

*Subcase both PAIR.*  
Then \(\tau(M)=\mathrm{PAIR}\), so \(d_T(M)=1\) and \(M\) is not SEP-pure. Also \(d_T(P)=d_T(Q)=1\). Hence \(\operatorname{diff}=0\), matching the claim.

*Subcase one PAIR, one SEP.*  
Without loss of generality \(\tau(P)=\mathrm{PAIR}\) and \(\tau(Q)\) discrete. Then \(\tau(M)\) is discrete, \(d_T(P)=1\), and \(d_T(Q),d_T(M)\in\{0,2\}\) according to purity.

- If \(Q\) is SEP-pure, each block of \(Q\) on \(S\) is a singleton; intersecting with the blocks of \(P\) forces \(M\) SEP-pure as well. Then \(\operatorname{diff}=1+0-0-1=0\).
- If \(Q\) is SEP-ext and \(M\) is SEP-pure, \(\operatorname{diff}=1+2-0-1=2\).
- If \(Q\) is SEP-ext and \(M\) is SEP-ext, \(\operatorname{diff}=1+2-2-1=0\).

All three possibilities agree with the claimed rule. (The combination “\(Q\) pure and \(M\) ext” is impossible.)

---

#### Case \(c_S=3\)

Then both \(\tau(P)\) and \(\tau(Q)\) are discrete, so \(\tau(M)\) is discrete as well and \(d_T(P),d_T(Q)\in\{0,2\}\).

**Subcase at least one input SEP-pure.**  
Suppose \(P\) is SEP-pure. Its blocks on \(S\) are exactly the singletons. The meet blocks are therefore
\[
M_s=P_s\cap Q_s=\{s\}\cap Q_s=\{s\},
\]
so \(M\) is SEP-pure. The claimed rule then predicts \(\operatorname{diff}=0\) (one input is pure). Direct substitution of the four-way values confirms the numerical value is indeed \(0\).

**Subcase both inputs SEP-ext.**  
Now \(d_T(P)=d_T(Q)=2\) and
\[
\operatorname{diff}=1-d_T(M)+(c_G-d_T(U)).
\]
The possible values of \(\tau(U)\) are discrete (\(c_G=3\)), PAIR (\(c_G=2\)) or ALL3 (\(c_G=1\)).

- PAIR or ALL3: the four-way table immediately gives \(d_T(U)=c_G-1\).
- Discrete (\(c_G=3\)): at least one of \(P,Q\) is SEP-ext, so at least one local block carries a nonempty external set \(E\). That set is inherited by the corresponding block of the coarser partition \(U\). Since \(\tau(U)\) remains discrete, that block cannot be a singleton. Hence \(U\) itself is SEP-ext and \(d_T(U)=2=c_G-1\).

In every subcase
\[
d_T(U)=c_G-1,
\]
and therefore
\[
\operatorname{diff}=2-d_T(M),
\]
which equals \(2\) precisely when \(M\) is SEP-pure and equals \(0\) when \(M\) is SEP-ext — again the claimed rule.

---

All branches are exhausted. This completes the proof.

### 9. One-sentence summary

Under the fixed 3-cycle on a three-point support, the difference between the orbit-join defect \(\Delta_d\) and the local-to-global block-count discrepancy \(c_S-c_G\) is completely determined by a purity condition on the meet: it equals 2 precisely when the meet is SEP-pure while both inputs fail to be SEP-pure, and equals 0 in every other case; the proof is a finite case analysis on the five-element partition lattice of the support, independent of ambient size \(n\ge 3\).
